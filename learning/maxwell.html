Maxwell Earth Model

From constitutive equations to time-dependent surface deformation

01 · Physical principles of Maxwell rheology

The Maxwell model describes the time evolution of stress and strain inside a material, not surface displacement directly. In the Earth, it is used to model the delayed mantle response to glacier retreat, glacial isostatic adjustment and earthquake stress changes. A spring and a viscous dashpot in series experience the same shear stress, while their strains add.

\[\gamma_e=\frac{\tau}{\mu},\qquad \dot\gamma_v=\frac{\tau}{\eta},\qquad \gamma=\gamma_e+\gamma_v.\]\[\boxed{\dot\gamma=\frac{\dot\tau}{\mu}+\frac{\tau}{\eta}}\]
SymbolMeaningUnit
\(\tau\)Shear stressPa
\(\gamma\)Engineering shear strainDimensionless
\(\mu\)Shear modulusPa
\(\eta\)Dynamic viscosityPa s
\(t\)Times

The elastic component controls the instantaneous response, while viscosity controls the rate of long-term flow. See also Elasticity & Viscoelasticity for comparative creep and recovery experiments.

02 · Three-dimensional constitutive equations

Decompose the stress and strain tensors into volumetric and deviatoric parts. With tensile stress positive and hydrostatic pressure positive in compression,

\[\boldsymbol\sigma=-p\mathbf I+\mathbf s,\qquad \boldsymbol\varepsilon=\frac{\varepsilon_v}{3}\mathbf I+\mathbf e,\qquad \varepsilon_v=\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol\varepsilon.\]

A commonly used Maxwell Earth has elastic bulk response and viscoelastic shear response. The deviatoric equation contains a factor of two because tensor shear strain is half engineering shear strain.

\[p=-K\varepsilon_v,\qquad \boxed{\dot{\mathbf s}+\frac{\mu}{\eta}\mathbf s=2\mu\dot{\mathbf e}.}\]

In a complete continuum calculation these equations are coupled to displacement compatibility, force balance, gravity perturbations and appropriate surface/interface conditions. The local constitutive law alone cannot determine a displacement field.

03 · Material stress relaxation and Maxwell time

After a sudden strain step, hold the strain fixed. The stress obeys a first-order decay equation, whose solution defines the material Maxwell time.

\[\dot\gamma=0\ \Longrightarrow\ \dot\tau=-\frac{\mu}{\eta}\tau,\qquad \boxed{\tau(t)=\tau_0 e^{-t/\tau_M},\quad\tau_M=\frac{\eta}{\mu}.}\]

4.2 · Correspondence principle

Under zero initial perturbation, Laplace transformation with variable \(s\) converts the Maxwell law into an equivalent elastic constitutive relation.

\[\begin{aligned}s\widetilde\gamma&=\frac{s\widetilde\tau}{\mu}+\frac{\widetilde\tau}{\eta},\\\widetilde\tau&=\frac{\mu s}{s+\mu/\eta}\widetilde\gamma,\\\boxed{\widetilde\mu(s)}&=\boxed{\frac{\mu s\tau_M}{1+s\tau_M}}.\end{aligned}\]

Use the transformed shear modulus in the radial elastic-gravitational boundary-value equations at each harmonic degree. The bulk modulus remains elastic in this formulation. Peltier (1974) and subsequent normal-mode theory provide the foundation of this approach.

\[\{\rho,K,\mu,\eta\}\ \longrightarrow\ \widetilde\mu(s)\ \longrightarrow\ \{\widetilde h_n,\widetilde l_n,\widetilde k_n\}\ \longrightarrow\ \{h_n(t),l_n(t),k_n(t)\}.\]

The last step uses numerical inverse Laplace transformation or normal-mode methods. For a transfer Love number \(\widetilde h_n(s)\), the transform of the unit-step response is \(\widetilde h_n(s)/s\). Thus viscosity enters at the Love-number calculation stage, not as an arbitrary multiplier on the final elastic displacement.

4.3 · Time-dependent Love numbers

\[h_n^{\rm step}(t)=h_n^E+\sum_{i=1}^{N_n}A_{ni}(1-e^{-t/T_{ni}}).\]

The elastic Love number is the instantaneous response. Each mode has amplitude \(A_{ni}\) and relaxation time \(T_{ni}\), determined by the boundary-value problem; mode amplitudes can have either sign. 05 · Historical glacier unloading and space–time convolution

Suppose annual mass increments are available from 1870 through 2025. The response in 2025 includes all earlier increments with their own elapsed times.

\[\begin{aligned}u(2025)&=\Delta M_{1870}G_u(\psi,155)\\&+\Delta M_{1871}G_u(\psi,154)+\cdots+\Delta M_{2025}G_u(\psi,0).\end{aligned}\]\[\boxed{u_i(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\rm cell}}\sum_{t_k\le t}\Delta M_j(t_k)G_u(\psi_{ij},t-t_k)}\]

Use mass increments, not repeatedly accumulated mass anomalies. Include earlier load history or a physically justified initial state.

Interactive constant-rate retreat and cessation

06 · Direct time-domain integration

An alternative to solving time-dependent Love numbers is to update stress within each material element at successive time steps. Assuming constant deviatoric strain rate during a step, integrate the Maxwell law exactly:

\[\boxed{\mathbf s_{k+1}=e^{-\Delta t/\tau_M}\mathbf s_k+2\mu\tau_M(1-e^{-\Delta t/\tau_M})\dot{\mathbf e}_k.}\]

The first term relaxes existing stress and the second introduces stress due to new deformation. A complete solver must recalculate the coupled displacement field so that force balance and boundary conditions are satisfied at each step. RELAX is an example of a numerical framework for time-dependent deformation.

MATLAB example: constant engineering shear strain rate

clear; clc;
mu = 60e9;          % Pa
eta = 1e19;         % Pa s
secyr = 365.25 * 86400;
tauM = eta / mu;
t_year = 0:0.05:30;
t = t_year * secyr;
gamma_dot = 1e-6 / secyr;
stress = zeros(size(t));

for k = 2:length(t)
    dt = t(k) - t(k-1);
    decay = exp(-dt/tauM);
    stress(k) = decay*stress(k-1) ...
        + mu*tauM*(1-decay)*gamma_dot;
end
stress_exact = eta*gamma_dot .* (1-exp(-t/tauM));

figure('Color','w');
plot(t_year,stress/1e6,'b-','LineWidth',2);
hold on;
plot(t_year,stress_exact/1e6,'r--','LineWidth',1.5);
xlabel('Time (years)');
ylabel('Shear stress (MPa)');
legend('Time stepping','Analytical solution','Location','best');
set(gca,'FontName','DejaVu Sans');
box on; grid on;

07 · A physical surface-displacement example

Consider a homogeneous incompressible Maxwell half-space with a linear gravitational restoring traction at its surface and an imposed sinusoidal pressure load. Neglect curvature, internal self-gravity and lithospheric bending. Define horizontal wavenumber \(q=2\pi/L\) and restoring stiffness \(B=\Delta\rho g\).

\[\widetilde u(s)=-\frac{\widetilde p(s)}{B+2q\widetilde\mu(s)}.\]

For a step pressure \(p_0\), substitution of the Maxwell modulus gives the instantaneous and equilibrium displacements and the surface relaxation time:

\[u_E=-\frac{p_0}{B+2\mu q},\qquad u_\infty=-\frac{p_0}{B},\]\[\boxed{T_R=\frac{\eta}{\mu}+\frac{2\eta q}{B}},\qquad \boxed{u(t)=u_E+(u_\infty-u_E)(1-e^{-t/T_R}).}\]

This explicitly separates material and surface relaxation. At a given viscosity the shorter wavelength has a larger relaxation time in this idealized model. The pressure amplitude is \(\rho_w g\Delta H\), and the spatial displacement is \(u(t)\cos(qx)\).

Combine reconstructed historical glacier mass change with contemporary GRACE and altimetry constraints, then calculate elastic and delayed responses to compare with GNSS and InSAR over stable bedrock.
