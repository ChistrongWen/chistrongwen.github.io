01 · Measurement physics

Newtonian gravity and changing mass

Earth's gravitational acceleration depends on its mass distribution. Ice loss, groundwater depletion, ocean mass redistribution and solid-Earth processes perturb the gravitational potential. GRACE (launched 2002) and GRACE-FO (launched 2018) observe these perturbations with two satellites following approximately the same orbit.

\[V(\mathbf r)=G\int\frac{\rho(\mathbf r')}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|}\,\mathrm dV',\qquad \mathbf g=\nabla V\]

Conceptual geometry, not to scale. Both satellites accelerate under gravity, but their accelerations differ as they pass a mass anomaly. The separation first changes and then evolves as the trailing spacecraft passes the same feature.

\[\rho=|\mathbf r_2-\mathbf r_1|,\qquad \dot\rho=\mathbf e_{12}\cdot(\mathbf v_2-\mathbf v_1)\]

Range acceleration contains the projected differential acceleration and a geometric velocity term. The microwave K/Ka-band ranging system measures inter-satellite range variations; GRACE-FO also carries a laser ranging interferometer. GPS determines the orbits, accelerometers estimate non-gravitational forces, and star cameras determine attitude. Gravity coefficients are estimated by fitting a dynamical orbit and ranging model, not by directly converting a single range change into water thickness.

02 · From gravity to mass

Spherical harmonic representation

The external potential is represented by fully normalized spherical harmonics. Colatitude is \(\theta\), longitude is \(\lambda\), \(a\) is the reference radius, and \(\bar C\) and \(\bar S\) are dimensionless coefficients.

\[\begin{aligned}V(r,\theta,\lambda)&=\frac{GM}{r}\sum_l\left(\frac ar\right)^l\sum_m \bar P_{lm}(\cos\theta)\\[-2pt]&\qquad{}\times\big[\bar C_{lm}\cos(m\lambda)+\bar S_{lm}\sin(m\lambda)\big]\end{aligned}\]

Subtract a consistent reference mean to obtain monthly coefficient anomalies. The radial attenuation factor \((a/r)^l\) suppresses short-wavelength signals at satellite altitude. The inverse problem amplifies high-degree noise.

Equivalent water height (EWH)

\[\begin{aligned}\Delta h(\theta,\lambda)&=\frac{a\bar\rho_e}{3\rho_w}\sum_l\frac{2l+1}{1+k'_l}\sum_m\bar P_{lm}(\cos\theta)\\[-2pt]&\qquad{}\times\big[\Delta\bar C_{lm}\cos(m\lambda)+\Delta\bar S_{lm}\sin(m\lambda)\big]\end{aligned}\]

Here \(\bar\rho_e\) is mean Earth density, \(\rho_w=1000\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\), and \(k'_l\) is the load Love number. The factor \(1/(1+k'_l)\) accounts for elastic gravitational response in a surface-loading interpretation. Use a consistent normalization, degree-1 reference convention and Earth model. Degree zero is normally not recovered as a time-variable global mass term. EWH expresses surface mass per unit area, not necessarily actual water depth.

\[\Delta\sigma=\rho_w\Delta h,\qquad \Delta M=\int\Delta\sigma\,\mathrm dA\]

Ice, water and solid-Earth mass redistribution may contribute to the same gravity observation. Independent information is needed to separate them.

03 · Data processing workflow

Level-1 to Level-3

  1. Level-1B: calibrated ranging, GPS orbit, accelerometer and attitude observations.
  2. Level-2: dynamical gravity inversion, with atmospheric/ocean de-aliasing, tides and other background models.
  3. Choose CSR, JPL or GFZ monthly SH solutions and a common maximum degree (e.g. 60 for a teaching workflow). For mascons use a documented Level-3 product instead.
  4. Construct monthly anomalies relative to the same reference period. Restore degree-1/geocenter coefficients, replace \(C_{20}\) and applicable \(C_{30}\) using release-specific SLR technical notes; match conventions and dates.
  5. Remove GIA or other modeled non-target solid-Earth contributions when appropriate. For regional ice unloading, a standard GIA model may omit local recent viscoelastic rebound.
  6. For SH, apply a documented decorrelation filter (e.g. P4M6) and Gaussian smoothing (e.g. 300 km); evaluate signal attenuation and leakage. Transform to EWH using load Love numbers.
  7. For mascons, verify already-applied corrections, coastline treatment and any recommended gain factors. Do not repeat SH corrections blindly.
  8. For ocean mass, check whether the relevant de-aliasing ocean field has been restored and whether atmospheric pressure terms need removal for the intended comparison.
  9. Mask study region, integrate cell areas, estimate trends and seasonal cycles; assess errors, spatial covariance and leakage. Preserve missing months, including the GRACE/GRACE-FO gap.

Area-weighted regional integration

\[\begin{aligned} A_i&=R^2\Delta\lambda\big[\sin(\phi_{\mathrm{north}})-\sin(\phi_{\mathrm{south}})\big],\\ \Delta M&=\rho_w\sum_i f_iA_i\Delta h_i\end{aligned}\]

Angles in radians; \(f_i\) is the fractional overlap between a grid cell and the region. A 1 cm EWH anomaly over 100,000 km² represents 1 Gt. For a rectangular region the explorer computes fractional overlap on a spherical Earth; research-grade integration should follow the product's geometry, masks and uncertainty conventions.

05 · Uncertainty and interpretation

GRACE observes large-scale gravity variations, not individual hydrological components. Major limitations include correlated coefficient errors, leakage, attenuation, temporal aliasing, geocenter and SLR uncertainty, GIA, recent viscoelastic deformation and imperfect atmosphere/ocean models. Estimate regional uncertainty with spatial covariance, not independent-pixel assumptions. Inverse modeling can recover a regional load using a spatial prior but is sensitive to model assumptions.

For glacier studies, combine altimetry and glacier outlines with GRACE and GNSS. For sea-level budgets, combine ocean mass with steric height and solid-Earth contributions in consistent reference frames. A global integrated anomaly depends on the product's degree-zero convention and background signals.
