From electromagnetic waves to crustal deformation

01 · Physical principles

Phase and initial phase

Phase describes the state of a periodic oscillation relative to a reference. Initial phase is its value at \(x=0\) and \(t=0\). Changing initial phase shifts the waveform without changing its amplitude, frequency or wavelength.

\[E(x,t)=A\cos(\omega t-kx+\phi_0),\qquad k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\]

Dashed: zero initial phase. Blue: shifted cosine. The phasor horizontal projection gives the instantaneous cosine value.

Superposition and interference

Two coherent waves of equal frequency and polarization can interfere. Their fields add; the resultant amplitude is \(2A|\cos(\Delta\phi/2)|\) for equal amplitudes. A stable relative phase produces stable interference.

\[I=I_1+I_2+2\sqrt{I_1I_2}\cos(\Delta\phi)\]

Why the radar phase contains \(\frac{4\pi R}{\lambda}\)

One wavelength corresponds to \(2\pi\) radians. Monostatic radar travels to the scatterer and back, so the path length is \(2R\). In an ideal coherent receiver the delayed carrier is multiplied by the conjugate reference carrier, removing its time dependence and initial phase.

\[\tau=\frac{2R}{c}\quad\longrightarrow\quad \exp(-i2\pi f\tau)\quad\longrightarrow\quad\phi=-\frac{4\pi R}{\lambda}\]

With the convention \(S_2S_1^*\) and positive displacement toward the satellite, \(\Delta\phi=4\pi d/\lambda\). A full phase cycle corresponds to \(\lambda/2\) in LOS displacement.

Complex SAR signals and digital interferometry

An SLC pixel stores \(S=I+iQ=A\exp(i\phi)\). A GRD intensity image does not retain phase. Repeat-pass InSAR does not require the two electromagnetic waves to physically meet: it multiplies the recorded complex samples after coregistration.

\[S_2S_1^*=A_1A_2\exp\!\big[i(\phi_2-\phi_1)\big]\]

Stable scattering makes the unknown scattering phase approximately cancel. Surface changes cause decorrelation and reduce phase reliability.

Wrapped phase, fringes and unwrapping

The measured argument is modulo \(2\pi\). Unwrapping estimates the missing integer cycles using spatial or temporal consistency. A fringe cycle represents \(\lambda/2\) of LOS displacement only after other phase terms are removed.

\[\phi_{\mathrm{true}}=\phi_{\mathrm{wrapped}}+2\pi n\]

02 · Observation geometry

Ascending and descending LOS projections

InSAR measures the projection of three-dimensional displacement onto a unit vector pointing from the ground toward the radar, not vertical displacement alone. For right-looking radar with heading \(\alpha\) clockwise from north and incidence \(\theta\) measured from vertical:

\[d=-E\sin\theta\cos\alpha+N\sin\theta\sin\alpha+U\cos\theta\]

Two viewing geometries cannot uniquely recover three components. Use GNSS or a physical model to constrain northward motion, or directly project modeled ENU displacement into LOS.

Spatial baseline and DEM error

Different satellite positions produce different ranges even for stationary terrain. Precise orbit and DEM information remove the modeled flat-earth and topographic contributions. A residual height error produces a phase error proportional to perpendicular baseline.

\[\begin{aligned}|\delta d|&\approx\frac{|B_\perp\delta h|}{R\sin\theta},\\ h_{\mathrm{amb}}&=\frac{\lambda R\sin\theta}{2|B_\perp|}\end{aligned}\]

Illustrative C-band geometry: \(R=800\,\mathrm{km},\ \theta=35^\circ\).

03 · Processing workflow

  1. Acquire compatible SLC images, precise orbits and DEM.
  2. Coregister images; refine TOPS burst alignment where applicable.
  3. Build a connected interferogram network using temporal and perpendicular baselines.
  4. Form complex interferograms and remove modeled flat-earth and topographic phase.
  5. Estimate coherence, multilook and filter phase.
  6. Unwrap phase and inspect connected components and closure residuals.
  7. Invert the network for displacement time series; correct atmosphere, DEM residuals and orbit errors.
  8. Geocode, establish reference datum, estimate velocity and validate with GNSS.

Processing order varies by software. SNAP, ISCE and GMTSAR generate interferograms; MintPy performs time-series analysis. ASF HyP3 provides cloud-generated interferograms.

SBAS time-series inversion

For an interferogram between acquisitions \(i\) and \(j\), \(D_{ij}=d_j-d_i+\varepsilon_{ij}\). Fix the first date and solve the connected network by weighted least squares. PS methods emphasize persistent scatterers; DS methods exploit statistically homogeneous distributed scatterers.

\[\hat{\mathbf d}=(\mathbf A^{\mathsf T}\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf A)^{-1}\mathbf A^{\mathsf T}\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf b\]

Estimate linear trends and seasonal terms, accounting for temporal and spatial correlation. A shared acquisition can introduce correlated errors across interferograms.

04 · Error sources and uncertainty

\[\phi_{\mathrm{int}}=\phi_{\mathrm{flat}}+\phi_{\mathrm{topo}}+\phi_{\mathrm{def}}+\phi_{\mathrm{atm}}+\phi_{\mathrm{orb}}+\phi_{\mathrm{noise}}\]

Tropospheric delay arises from atmospheric refractivity; ionospheric effects are dispersive. ERA5-based corrections and split-spectrum methods can help. Avoid removing genuine long-wavelength deformation through indiscriminate deramping or phase–elevation regression.

Solid Earth tides, ocean tidal loading, atmospheric loading and hydrological loading are real displacement signals rather than propagation errors. Their treatment depends on the research target.

Coherence and random phase noise

\[\sigma_\phi\approx\sqrt{\frac{1-\gamma^2}{2L\gamma^2}}\]

High-coherence approximation for random noise only; excludes atmosphere, orbit errors and unwrapping failures.

05 · Geophysical interpretation

Glacier unloading and mantle rheology

At Kerguelen Island, a proposed research strategy is to combine glacier mass histories constrained by GRACE and satellite altimetry with GNSS and InSAR over stable bedrock. Predict elastic and delayed viscoelastic displacement, project ENU into each radar LOS, and reference the predictions in space and time exactly as the observations. Fit the joint data using their covariance and evaluate trade-offs between load history, lithosphere thickness and mantle viscosity. InSAR alone cannot uniquely determine mantle water content or viscosity.

\[\tau_M=\frac{\eta}{\mu}\]

A Maxwell material timescale, not the full surface response of a layered self-gravitating Earth.

InSAR is relative to a spatial reference and a reference date. GNSS can constrain the offset; one station cannot independently determine a two-dimensional ramp. Actual Kerguelen coverage and coherence require evaluation.
