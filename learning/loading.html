Elastic Loading

From surface mass redistribution to vertical crustal displacement

Learn the physical theory, construct load Green functions from PREM load Love numbers, and calculate elastic displacement from spatial mass distributions. All prescribed loads are educational examples, not observed uplift estimates.

01 · Physical principles

Why does surface mass deform the Earth?

Glacier melting, groundwater, reservoirs, atmospheric pressure and ocean mass redistribute mass at the surface. Their pressure and gravitational forcing deform the solid Earth. Elastic loading is the instantaneous response; additional mass generally produces local subsidence and mass loss produces local uplift. Far-field response may change sign in a layered, self-gravitating sphere.

Conceptual cross-section, deformation exaggerated. Dashed horizontal line: original surface.

\[\Delta\sigma=\rho_w\Delta h\quad[\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}}],\qquad \Delta p=g\Delta\sigma\quad[\mathrm{Pa}]\]

Use \(\rho_w=1000\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\) for equivalent water height (EWH). For actual ice thickness changes, use an appropriate ice/firn density. Upward displacement and mass gain are positive. Distinguish instantaneous elastic deformation from delayed viscoelastic rebound and tectonic motion.

02 · Load Green functions

Point-load response and superposition

A load Green function is the displacement at an observation point due to a unit mass at another point. For a spherical, radially stratified, linear elastic Earth it depends only on angular separation \(\psi\). Load Love numbers include the response to surface pressure and gravitational forcing.

\[\begin{aligned}\cos\psi&=\sin\phi\sin\phi'+\cos\phi\cos\phi'\cos(\lambda-\lambda'),\\ G_u(\psi)&=\frac a{M_e}\sum_{n=0}^\infty h'_nP_n(\cos\psi)\quad[\mathrm{m\,kg^{-1}}]\end{aligned}\]

Here \(a\) and \(M_e\) are Earth radius and mass; \(P_n\) is the ordinary Legendre polynomial with \(P_n(1)=1\). This kernel is normalized per unit mass. Do not multiply by gravity, \(4\pi\) or area again inside it. Some published tables are normalized per unit force or multiplied by angular-distance factors; always check units.

\[\begin{gathered}P_0(x)=1,\qquad P_1(x)=x,\\ P_{n+1}(x)=\frac{(2n+1)xP_n(x)-nP_{n-1}(x)}{n+1}\end{gathered}\]
\[\begin{aligned}u(P,t)&=\int G_u(\psi(P,Q))\,\Delta\sigma(Q,t)\,\mathrm dA(Q)\\&\approx\sum_j\bar G_j\Delta M_j(t),\\ \Delta M_j&=\rho_w\Delta h_jA_j,\\ \bar G_j&=\frac1{A_j}\int_{\mathrm{cell}\ j}G_u(\psi)\,\mathrm dA\end{aligned}\]

For a regular spherical latitude-longitude grid, \(A_j=a^2\Delta\lambda(\sin\phi_{\mathrm{north}}-\sin\phi_{\mathrm{south}})\), with angles in radians. The point kernel is singular at zero separation, but the finite-area integral is finite. Use cell-averaged kernels or accurate quadrature for stations near or inside load cells. Include remote loads when required. For research use high-degree Love numbers and a converged or asymptotically regularized kernel, not a raw truncated sum.

03 · Load Love numbers and spectral truncation

PREM: Han & Wahr (1995)

Load Love numbers \(h',\,l'\) and \(k'\) describe vertical displacement, horizontal displacement and induced gravitational potential. They are dimensionless and differ from tidal Love numbers. The actual PREM table here has degrees 0–696, obtained from gravity-toolkit. The distributed degree-one values are in the CM convention (\(k'_1=0\)). CF is obtained using \(\alpha=(h'_1+2l'_1)/3\) and \(h'_1\to h'_1-\alpha\). Other degree-one coefficients require the corresponding shift if used for other observables.

04 · Loading calculation workflow

kg→Earth model
Love numbers→Green function
m/kg→Cell integration→Displacement
mm
  1. Choose the load data, reference period, time span and mask. Check GRACE corrections and whether the gravity anomaly is interpretable as surface mass.
  2. Convert EWH, water levels or ice thickness into signed mass using appropriate density and area. Use actual lake volume changes or inundation geometry when the shoreline changes.
  3. Choose Earth structure and reference frame. Obtain high-degree load Love numbers from a documented solver such as LoadDef.
  4. Compute the angular Green function and check units, spectral convergence and near-field behavior.
  5. For every station and cell, compute distance and integrate the kernel over nearby cells. Sum all contributions in meters, then convert to millimeters.
  6. Repeat for each epoch, estimate trend and seasonality, propagate uncertainty and compare against GNSS in a consistent frame. Include compensating ocean mass and remote loads as appropriate.

For a fixed elastic Earth and fixed load geometry, \(\mathbf u(t)=\mathbf H\mathbf m(t)\). Mass trends may be propagated directly if epochs and estimators are consistent. Viscoelastic rebound requires historical forcing and temporal convolution, not only today's load multiplied by an elastic kernel.

05 · Interactive spherical-Earth experiment

Uniform spherical cap — finite-load convolution

The point Green function is integrated analytically over a uniform cap using the Legendre addition theorem. This avoids sampling a singular point at the load center. The result remains band-limited to the selected maximum degree.

\[\begin{gathered}\beta=\frac{r_{\mathrm{load}}}{a},\quad c=\cos\beta,\quad A_{\mathrm{cap}}=2\pi a^2(1-c),\\ F_0=1,\qquad F_n=\frac{P_{n-1}(c)-P_{n+1}(c)}{(2n+1)(1-c)},\\ u(\psi)=\frac{a\Delta M}{M_e}\sum_n h'_nF_nP_n(\cos\psi)\end{gathered}\]

06 · Interactive local near-field experiment

Finite circular load on an elastic half-space

For a homogeneous isotropic elastic half-space, the Boussinesq solution for a downward point force \(F\) gives \(u=-(1-\nu^2)F/(\pi Er)\). Substituting \(F=g\Delta M\) gives the local mass Green function. This excludes layering, spherical geometry and self-gravitation; it is a near-field benchmark, not PREM.

\[\begin{aligned}G_u(r)&=-\frac{(1-\nu^2)g}{\pi Er},\\ u(r)&=-\frac{(1-\nu^2)g\Delta\sigma}{\pi E}\int_{\mathrm{disk}}\frac{\mathrm dA'}{|r-r'|},\\ u(0)&=-\frac{2(1-\nu^2)g\Delta\sigma R_{\mathrm{load}}}{E}\end{aligned}\]

In station-centered polar coordinates, the area element \(s\,\mathrm ds\,\mathrm d\alpha\) cancels the \(1/s\) singularity. Integrate the radial chord length analytically and then integrate over angle. The center has a closed-form solution.

07 · Qinghai Lake water-level loading

From lake-level change to a spatial deformation field

Change the water level of Qinghai Lake and explore the instantaneous elastic response of the surrounding crust. The default shoreline is an approximate educational polygon, not a surveyed boundary. Import your own WGS84 GeoJSON Polygon or MultiPolygon for a more accurate fixed shoreline. Water-level change is uniform within the selected lake footprint; shoreline migration is neglected.

\[\begin{gathered}\Delta\sigma=\rho_w\Delta H,\qquad \Delta M=\rho_w A\Delta H,\\ u(P)=-\frac{(1-\nu^2)g}{\pi E}\int_{\mathrm{lake}}\frac{\Delta\sigma(Q)}{|P-Q|}\,\mathrm dA\end{gathered}\]
