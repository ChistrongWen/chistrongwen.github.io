Step offsets and long-term trends in geodetic time series

How discontinuities affect estimates of secular change: an academic expansion of a MATLAB demonstration.

1. Research question

Discontinuities in lake-level, GNSS, or other geodetic time series may arise from instrumental changes, reference-frame updates, earthquakes, or real physical events. A step omitted from a trend model can be partially absorbed into the estimated linear rate. This note explains the effect without treating the step as a change in the underlying secular rate.

2. Observation model

For observations y at epochs t, use a constant, a linear term, annual and semiannual harmonics, and a known event-step indicator:

y(t) = a + bt + Σk=1,2 [ck cos(2πkt) + dk sin(2πkt)] + s H(t − t0) + ε(t)

Here b is the secular trend, s is the signed step amplitude, and H is zero before the event and one from the event onward. Centering the time coordinate around a reference epoch can improve numerical conditioning. The sign of s must be defined consistently.

3. Weighted least-squares estimation

When observation standard deviations σi are supplied, a diagonal approximation uses W = diag(1/σi2). For design matrix X containing the trend and seasonal columns, the weighted least-squares estimate is:

β̂ = (XᵀWX)−1XᵀWy

For a known step amplitude s, the step-corrected observation is ycorr = y − sH. Thus the difference between fitted trends is determined analytically by the projection of the step indicator onto the trend column:

Δβ̂ = −s (XᵀWX)−1XᵀWH

Important: If the step is unknown, estimate it as an additional model parameter rather than correcting with an assumed value. A step and trend can be strongly correlated, especially with short or uneven temporal coverage.

% Minimal MATLAB structure: signed step s is known
% t = decimal years; y = observations; sigma = standard deviation
H = double(t >= eventYear);
tau = t(:) - mean(t);
X = [ones(numel(t),1), tau, ...
     cos(2*pi*t(:)), sin(2*pi*t(:)), ...
     cos(4*pi*t(:)), sin(4*pi*t(:))];
w = 1 ./ sigma(:).^2;
A = X' * (w .* X);
betaRaw = A \ (X' * (w .* y(:)));
betaCorrected = A \ (X' * (w .* (y(:)-s*H(:))));
deltaTrend = betaCorrected(2)-betaRaw(2);

The example reports a trend in the units of the input observable per year. In MATLAB, use vectorized weights rather than forming a dense diagonal matrix for large data sets.

4. Diagnostics and limitations

  • Event definition: check step date and signed amplitude independently of the trend fit.
  • Temporal sampling: gaps or asymmetric coverage before/after the event change the coupling between step and rate.
  • Noise: diagonal WLS does not account for temporally correlated noise; trend uncertainty should be re-evaluated where colored noise is important.
  • Uncertainty: propagate uncertainty in the step amplitude; naive standard errors conditional on a fixed step can be too small.
  • Validation: compare no-step, known-step and estimated-step fits with residual diagnostics and model sensitivity.

5. Source and reproducibility

This note is an academic adaptation, not a verbatim migration or an independently validated numerical result. The original CSDN article includes a longer observational example and MATLAB code.
